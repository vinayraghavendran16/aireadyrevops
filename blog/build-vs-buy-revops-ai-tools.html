Build vs Buy for RevOps AI Tools: A Decision Framework That Actually Helps

July 13, 2026 8 min read

The pitch from every AI vendor: "Our tool will transform your revenue operations in weeks." The pitch from your engineering team: "We can build this ourselves." Both are partially right. The decision deserves structured analysis.

When to buy

Buy when the use case is a solved problem for your industry. Lead scoring, forecasting, conversation intelligence, and email sequencing are all well-served by vendors with years of R&D. Also buy when speed matters: a vendor gets you to value in weeks, not months.

When to build

Build when the use case depends on proprietary data no vendor can access. Also build when the AI capability is a competitive differentiator. Build when integration requirements are so specific that a vendor tool would need extensive customization.

The three-year TCO model

For buying: year one license plus implementation, years two and three renewals, ongoing admin costs. For building: engineering hours, maintenance (25% of build cost per year), infrastructure, and opportunity cost.

The foundation prerequisite

Whether you build or buy, the AI tool is only as good as the data it operates on. The build-vs-buy decision is important, but it is downstream of the readiness question.

Frequently asked questions

When should a RevOps team build vs buy?

Build when the use case is unique to your business and requires proprietary data. Buy when the use case is common, the vendor has domain expertise you lack, and speed matters.

How do I calculate the true cost of building in-house?

Add engineering hours, ongoing maintenance (20-30% of build cost per year), infrastructure costs, and opportunity cost. Compare to three years of vendor licensing plus implementation.
