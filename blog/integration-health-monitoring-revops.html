Integration Health Monitoring for RevOps: Stop Finding Out About Sync Failures from Reps

Here is how most RevOps teams discover integration failures: a rep notices that a lead from a webinar last week never appeared in Salesforce. They mention it in a team meeting. Someone checks the marketing automation sync and discovers it has been failing for five days. Meanwhile, 200 leads are sitting in a queue that nobody is working.

What to monitor

For every integration that feeds your revenue stack, track four signals. Sync job completion: is the scheduled sync running without errors? Record volume: are the expected number of records syncing each day? Data freshness: when was the last successful sync? Error rates: what percentage of sync attempts are failing?

How to set it up

If you use an iPaaS (Workato, MuleSoft, Tray.io), it has built-in monitoring. Configure alerts to go to a RevOps Slack channel. For native integrations, build a daily scheduled Salesforce report that checks record counts and last-modified timestamps.

For critical-path integrations (anything that affects lead routing, pipeline, or revenue recognition), set a response SLA: failures must be investigated within 4 hours during business hours.

The AI angle

AI tools that aggregate data from multiple sources are especially sensitive to integration health. If your product usage data is two days stale because of a failed sync, an account intelligence agent will recommend actions based on old information.

Integration health monitoring is one of the criteria in the Integration Architecture dimension of the AI-Ready RevOps Framework. Without it, your AI tools are quietly learning from incomplete data.

Frequently asked questions

How do I monitor Salesforce integration health?

Set up daily checks on sync job completion, record counts (sudden drops indicate failures), field-level data freshness, and error logs from integration middleware.

What are common causes of integration failures in RevOps?

API rate limits, expired OAuth tokens, field mapping changes, data type mismatches, and vendor API deprecations are the most common causes.
