Lead-to-Cash Process Documentation: A Practical Guide for RevOps

April 01, 2026 8 min read

Ask your VP of Sales to walk you through your lead-to-cash process. Then ask your VP of Marketing. Then ask your head of finance. You will get three different stories. This is normal. It is also the single biggest obstacle to both operational efficiency and AI deployment.

What to document

A useful lead-to-cash document covers: how leads are captured and routed, what qualification criteria are used, how opportunities are created and staged, approval and discounting processes, how contracts are executed, how revenue is recognized, and how handoffs happen at each transition.

For each stage, document: the responsible team, entry and exit criteria, the systems involved, and the SLAs.

How to build the documentation

Bring one representative from marketing, sales, sales operations, customer success, and finance into a room for 90 minutes. Map the process from left to right. At each transition point, ask: "Who owns this handoff? What triggers it? What system records it? What happens when it fails?"

The disagreements that surface in this meeting are the most valuable output. Document them alongside the agreed process and resolve them in follow-up meetings.

Keeping it alive

Assign a RevOps owner. Review quarterly or whenever a major change happens. When you deploy AI tools, reference the process documentation as the source of truth for how the tool should behave.

Frequently asked questions

What is a lead-to-cash process?

Lead-to-cash covers the entire revenue lifecycle from initial lead capture through qualification, opportunity management, closing, invoicing, and revenue recognition.

Why does AI need documented processes?

AI agents that route leads or automate follow-ups need explicit process logic. If the process exists only in people's heads, the agent has nothing to execute against.
