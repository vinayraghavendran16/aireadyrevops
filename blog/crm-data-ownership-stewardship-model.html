CRM Data Ownership: Why 'Everyone Owns It' Means Nobody Does

When you ask who owns data quality in your CRM, the answer is usually "everyone" or "RevOps" or "the admin." None of these work. "Everyone" means nobody is accountable. "RevOps" means a stretched team adds another responsibility without capacity.

What a data stewardship model looks like

A data steward is the named person accountable for a specific data domain. The Account Data Steward monitors field completion rates, approves new custom fields, reviews picklist values quarterly, and escalates issues. This is 2-4 hours per week: one person, one domain, clear accountability.

How to implement

Map CRM objects to stewards. Account and Contact data: sales operations. Opportunity and Pipeline data: sales management or RevOps. Marketing data: marketing operations. CS data: CS operations.

For each steward, define: what they monitor, what thresholds trigger action (field completion below 80%, duplicate rate above 5%), and what authority they have.

The cadence

Monthly: check field completion rates, picklist distributions, duplicate counts. Quarterly: field inventory audit, integration data quality check, cross-domain alignment.

Data ownership is one of four criteria in the Data Foundation dimension of the AI-Ready RevOps Framework. When AI tools produce bad outputs, the first question is "who is accountable for the data quality?" If the answer is nobody, that is the root cause.

Frequently asked questions

What is a data steward?

A data steward is the named person accountable for the quality of a specific data domain. They define standards, monitor compliance, approve changes, and escalate issues.

How many data stewards does a mid-market company need?

3-5 covering the major CRM objects. These are responsibilities, not full-time roles.
